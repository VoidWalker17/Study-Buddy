# Week 1 Journal — Study Buddy

## Project Proposal & Research

Week 1 mainly focused on understanding the problem we wanted to solve and preparing the initial proposal for Study Buddy.

---

## Problem Identification

We looked at how most AI learning tools work and identified that they usually act as tutors. This can make students more passive because they mostly ask questions and read the answers.

Our idea was to reverse this approach using the **Feynman Technique**. In Study Buddy, the student teaches an AI learner instead of simply being taught by AI.

The main problems identified were:

* Students may not actively explain what they have learned.
* Normal AI models already have a lot of pretrained knowledge.
* It is difficult to objectively measure whether a student actually understands a topic.

---

## Proposed Solution

We proposed a two-agent system:

* **Teacher Agent** — has access to the uploaded study material and generates and evaluates tests.
* **Child Agent** — learns only from what the student teaches it.
* **Memory & Learning Engine** — stores the concepts and knowledge extracted from the student's explanation.

The basic idea is:

**Student teaches → Child Agent learns → Child Agent takes test → Teacher Agent evaluates**

---

## Project Scope

For the initial version, we planned to include:

* Study material upload.
* Teacher Agent processing.
* Text-based teaching.
* Memory extraction.
* Test generation.
* Automated evaluation.

Voice interaction and advanced analytics were kept as later features.

---

## Evaluation

We also discussed how we would measure the effectiveness of the system.

The main metric proposed was the **Teaching Efficacy Score (TES)**, which is based on the Child Agent's performance in the test generated by the Teacher Agent.

A cold baseline test was also included to check the Child Agent's knowledge before the student teaches it.

---

## Challenges

The main challenges during Week 1 were:

* Clearly defining the difference between the Teacher and Child Agents.
* Deciding how to prevent the Child Agent from accessing the source material.
* Keeping the project scope realistic.
* Finding a measurable way to evaluate student teaching.
